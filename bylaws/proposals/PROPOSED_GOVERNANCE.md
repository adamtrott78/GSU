# PROPOSED GSU GOVERNANCE STRUCTURE

**Status:** PROPOSAL FOR LEADERSHIP DISCUSSION — NOT ADOPTED  
**Prepared for:** Graduate Student Union leadership meeting, September 22, 2026  
**Local:** U-Mass Dartmouth Maintainers, Local 6350, AFT Massachusetts, AFL-CIO  
**Purpose:** Present one coherent governance model that can be approved, modified, or rejected by GSU leadership before review by the Local 6350 President and AFT/AFT Massachusetts.

---

## 1. Design objective

The goal is to give Graduate Student Employees real democratic self-governance inside Local 6350 without unnecessarily dismantling the existing Maintainers governance structure.

The proposal therefore uses a **two-layer model**:

1. **Local 6350 governance** remains the umbrella organization.
2. **Graduate Student Employees / GSU** becomes a formally recognized bargaining-unit chapter with its own elected officers and Representative Council.

This follows structures already used in other AFT locals:

- Chelsea Local 1340 uses bargaining-unit-specific vice presidents who sit on the Local Executive Board and participate in bargaining and grievances.
- Chelmsford Local 3569 uses one bargaining-unit vice president per bargaining unit and a Representative Council separate from the Executive Board.

The proposal deliberately takes the **compact Executive Board / separate Representative Council** approach so that broad GSU representation does not unintentionally alter the voting balance of the Local-wide Executive Board.

---

# 2. Proposed Local 6350 structure

## Local-wide elected officers

Preserve the four existing Local-wide offices:

- President
- Vice-President
- Treasurer
- Secretary

These remain officers of the Federation elected under the Local Constitution.

## Guaranteed GSU Local officer

Add:

- **Graduate Student Vice-President**

The GSU President would serve **ex officio** as the Graduate Student Vice-President of Local 6350.

This means one person has two linked roles:

- inside GSU: **GSU President**
- inside Local 6350: **Graduate Student Vice-President**

There is no separate election for the Local title. GSU members elect their President, and that elected President automatically becomes the Graduate Student Vice-President of Local 6350.

### Why this model

- gives GSU a guaranteed voting seat on the Local Executive Board;
- avoids creating a fifth separate GSU officer whose only function is Local representation;
- preserves the traditional President / Vice President / Treasurer / Secretary structure GSU leadership has discussed;
- resembles the bargaining-unit vice-president structures used in Chelsea and Chelmsford;
- minimizes disruption to the existing Maintainers officer structure.

---

# 3. Proposed Local 6350 Executive Board

The Local Executive Board should be specifically enumerated rather than defined as every person who happens to hold an elected title.

### Proposed voting members

- Local President
- Local Vice-President
- Local Treasurer
- Local Secretary
- Graduate Student Vice-President / GSU President
- the existing Local-wide committee chairs, preserving their current Executive Board status
- chief delegate to the local labor council, if current leadership wishes to preserve that seat

### What is NOT automatic

GSU Representative Council members do **not** automatically become Local 6350 Executive Board members.

This is intentional.

A Representative Council could eventually contain many college representatives. Automatically seating all of them on the Local Board could change the balance between bargaining units. The Representative Council should govern and represent GSU internally while the GSU President provides the formal GSU bridge to the Local Board.

---

# 4. Proposed GSU officers

The Graduate Student Employees Chapter elects four officers:

## President

Responsibilities:
- chief elected representative of GSU;
- presides at GSU membership and Representative Council meetings;
- serves ex officio as Graduate Student Vice-President of Local 6350;
- represents GSU on the Local Executive Board;
- serves as primary liaison between GSU and Local-wide leadership;
- participates in bargaining and grievance administration;
- reports Local Executive Board actions relevant to GSU back to GSU leadership and membership.

## Vice President

Responsibilities:
- performs President's duties when the President is absent;
- succeeds to President if that office becomes vacant;
- coordinates committees/caucuses and Representative Council operations;
- assists with bargaining, organizing, and contract enforcement.

## Treasurer

Responsibilities:
- tracks the GSU budget and approved unit expenditures;
- works with the Local Treasurer on financial reporting and reimbursements;
- reports regularly to the Representative Council and GSU membership;
- does not independently control Local 6350 bank accounts unless formally authorized.

## Secretary

Responsibilities:
- records and distributes GSU meeting minutes;
- maintains GSU governance records;
- issues notices required by GSU bylaws;
- maintains election and Representative Council records with the appropriate Local officers;
- supports member communications.

---

# 5. Proposed terms

## GSU officers

**One-year terms.**

Proposed term:
- July 1 through June 30

Proposed regular election:
- during the spring semester, sufficiently before July 1 to permit a transition period.

### Why one year

Graduate employment and enrollment create much faster turnover than in the comparator teacher locals.

Local 6350 currently uses three-year terms; Chelsea and Chelmsford use two-year terms. Neither is designed around graduate-worker turnover.

A one-year term:
- reduces vacancy risk;
- gives each incoming cohort a realistic route to leadership;
- creates an annual democratic mandate;
- still permits reelection while a member remains eligible.

---

# 6. Officer eligibility

### Proposed permanent rule

Any GSU member in good standing is eligible to run for a GSU office or Representative Council seat.

There is **no one-year waiting period** for GSU chapter office.

### Reason

The existing Local Constitution requires one year of membership before holding Local office. That rule is poorly matched to a workforce with short appointments and rapid turnover and would unnecessarily restrict the pool of eligible graduate-worker leaders.

The GSU President's Local role as Graduate Student Vice-President therefore requires a specific constitutional exception.

That exception is an explicit part of Draft 1 and should be approved, modified, or rejected by leadership with the rest of the proposed structure.

---

# 7. Temporary appointment gaps

### Proposed continuity rule

A temporary gap between covered Graduate Student Employees appointments — including a summer period without a covered UMass Dartmouth appointment — does **not** by itself terminate GSU membership or eligibility to continue serving in elected office when the member is expected to return to a covered Graduate Student Employees appointment in the immediately following academic term.

This rule is intended to cover ordinary funding and appointment gaps. It does not preserve eligibility after graduation, permanent departure from UMass Dartmouth, or where the member is not expected to return to a covered appointment.

Leadership should expressly approve, modify, or reject this rule.

# 21. GSU Representative Council

Create a Representative Council separate from the Local 6350 Executive Board.

## Purpose

The Council is the primary representative body for GSU between general membership meetings.

It:
- brings concerns from academic units to GSU leadership;
- coordinates organizing and communications;
- identifies contract-enforcement and grievance issues;
- provides input on bargaining priorities;
- creates committees/caucuses;
- makes recommendations to the GSU membership;
- carries out policies adopted by GSU members.

The Council does not replace the authority of the full GSU membership where the Constitution, chapter bylaws, law, or the collective bargaining agreement reserves a decision to the membership.

---

# 21. Proposed Representative Council composition

## Voting members

- GSU President
- GSU Vice President
- GSU Treasurer
- GSU Secretary
- elected academic-unit representatives
- two at-large representatives elected by the entire GSU membership

## Academic-unit representation

### Proposed hybrid formula

Each UMass Dartmouth college or school containing at least five GSU bargaining-unit members receives:

- **one guaranteed representative**, plus
- **one additional representative for each additional fifty GSU members assigned to that college/school.**

Example:

- 5–54 members → 1 representative
- 55–104 members → 2 representatives
- 105–154 members → 3 representatives

Members in an academic unit below the five-member threshold remain eligible to vote for the at-large representatives and participate in GSU committees/caucuses.

### Why a hybrid model

Pure one-college/one-vote representation can seriously underrepresent large colleges.

Pure proportional representation can eliminate meaningful representation for small colleges.

A guaranteed seat plus proportional additional seats addresses both problems.

### Apportionment

The GSU Secretary and Local membership records should be used to determine the number of seats before each regular election.

If reliable college/school membership data are not available for the initial election, the first election may use one representative per recognized college/school, with reapportionment beginning at the next regular election.

---

# 21. Cross-cutting representation

Do **not** create separate electoral districts for every overlapping graduate-worker identity.

Instead, authorize the Representative Council to create standing committees and caucuses, including as needed:

- Research Employees / RAs
- Teaching and Instructional Employees / TAs
- Master's Student Employees
- Doctoral Student Employees
- International Graduate Employees
- Organizing & Membership
- Contract Enforcement / Grievances
- Communications

### Reason

A member may simultaneously be:
- a PhD student;
- an RA;
- an international worker;
- in Engineering;
- a TA during another semester.

Using all of those identities as electoral districts would create overlapping constituencies and unclear accountability.

Academic college/school is proposed as the stable electoral base; committees/caucuses address cross-cutting interests.

---

# 21. Bargaining authority

The existing Local Constitution has a Collective Bargaining Committee designed specifically around Maintainers classifications.

The amended Constitution should expressly permit each bargaining unit to have its own bargaining committee.

## Proposed GSU rule

GSU shall select its own bargaining committee under its chapter bylaws.

The GSU bargaining committee:
- develops bargaining proposals with input from GSU members;
- represents the Graduate Student Employees bargaining unit in negotiations;
- reports tentative agreements to GSU members;
- does not bind another Local 6350 bargaining unit.

Contract ratification remains solely with the members of the bargaining unit covered by the agreement, consistent with existing Article XV.

---

# 21. Grievances and contract enforcement

The GSU President, Vice President, and trained representatives should work with the Local grievance structure on Graduate Student Employees matters.

Representative Council members should serve as first-line points of contact for:
- identifying possible contract violations;
- basic contract questions;
- benefits/workload/appointment concerns;
- routing grievances to the appropriate trained union representative.

A Representative Council member should not be required to independently process a formal grievance without appropriate training and authority.

---

# 21. Vacancies

## President

If the GSU President leaves office:
- the GSU Vice President becomes President for the remainder of the term;
- the new President automatically becomes Graduate Student Vice-President of Local 6350.

## Other officers

If another officer position becomes vacant with more than 90 days remaining in the term:
- a special election should be held.

If fewer than 90 days remain:
- the Representative Council may appoint an eligible member to serve for the remainder of the term.

## Representative Council seats

Vacancies should normally be filled by a special election of the affected constituency when more than 90 days remain.

The Council may appoint an eligible member for the remainder of a term when fewer than 90 days remain or when an election is impracticable during a University recess.

---

# 21. Initial transition election

The constitutional amendments should not force GSU to wait until the next ordinary Local triennial election.

### Proposed rule

Following adoption of the amendments:

1. an initial GSU nomination period opens within 14 days;
2. the initial GSU election occurs as soon as practicable after required election notice;
3. the elected officers and representatives take office immediately after certification of the results;
4. their initial terms end June 30, 2027;
5. regular annual spring elections begin in 2027.

The election provisions in Draft 1 now incorporate the AFT-required democratic-election safeguards, including secret ballot, mailed election notice for officer elections, candidate observer rights, and one-year record retention.

---

# 21. GSU membership meetings

GSU should be authorized to conduct its own chapter membership meetings in addition to Local-wide meetings.

Proposed chapter bylaws should permit:
- in-person meetings;
- electronic meetings;
- hybrid meetings.

Electronic participation must preserve:
- member identity;
- deliberation;
- voting integrity;
- secret ballots where required.

---

# 21. Financial structure

This proposal does **not** attempt to unilaterally redesign Local 6350 finances.

Proposed principle:

- Local funds remain under the Local Treasurer and Local Constitution.
- GSU may receive an approved annual budget or allocation.
- The GSU Treasurer tracks that allocation and provides reports.
- GSU expenditures follow Local financial controls unless the Constitution expressly delegates additional authority.

Draft 1 preserves the existing Local dues ceiling and applies the same full, one-half, one-quarter, and one-eighth income-based rate structure to Graduate Student Employees, using total UMass Dartmouth fiscal-year compensation across fall, spring, and summer to determine the applicable class.

---

# 21. Contract ratification

Preserve existing Article XV's core rule:

> only members of the bargaining unit covered by the proposed agreement vote on ratification.

For a GSU contract:
- only eligible Graduate Student Employees vote;
- GSU leadership administers the unit-level process in coordination with Local officers;
- members of the Maintainers bargaining unit do not vote on the GSU agreement.

The reverse applies to a Maintainers agreement.

---

# 21. What remains Local-wide

GSU autonomy does not mean forming a separate Local.

Local-wide matters remain governed by Local 6350, including:
- AFT/AFT Massachusetts affiliation;
- Local-wide constitutional compliance;
- Local treasury and required financial reporting;
- Local-wide delegates;
- common legal/administrative obligations;
- issues expressly affecting more than one bargaining unit;
- amendment of the Local Constitution.

---

# 21. What GSU controls

Subject to the Local Constitution, AFT governing documents, applicable law, and the collective bargaining agreement, GSU should control its own:

- internal elections;
- officers;
- Representative Council;
- chapter meetings;
- committees/caucuses;
- bargaining priorities;
- bargaining committee selection;
- Graduate Student Employees contract negotiations;
- unit-level organizing;
- GSU communications;
- input into and administration of GSU grievances;
- contract-ratification process for the GSU bargaining unit;
- approved GSU budget.

---

# 21. Why this is the proposed Tuesday model

This proposal intentionally avoids two extremes.

## It does not make GSU an appointed committee

GSU officers and representatives are elected by GSU members and have defined authority.

## It does not create a separate union outside Local 6350

GSU remains a bargaining-unit chapter of the Local, has guaranteed Local representation, and works through common Local institutions where appropriate.

The design therefore aims for:

**one Local + distinct bargaining units + democratic unit self-governance + shared Local governance.**
