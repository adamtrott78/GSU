## Decision 3 — One-year GSU terms

### Proposal

GSU officers and Representative Council members serve annual terms, normally July 1 through June 30.

### Recommendation

### Why

Graduate-worker turnover is much faster than the workforces covered by the comparator constitutions. The Local's three-year term is poorly suited to graduate employment and would predictably create vacancies.

## Decision 3 — No one-year waiting period for GSU office

### Proposal

Any GSU member in good standing may run for GSU office or Representative Council.

The existing Local one-year membership requirement does not apply to GSU chapter office, .

### Recommendation

**Approve as proposed.**

### Why

A mandatory one-year waiting period substantially narrows leadership eligibility in a high-turnover bargaining unit and would make the initial GSU election impossible because the GSU itself has not existed for one year. The proposed amendment expressly creates the exception for GSU chapter office.

**Leadership decision:** APPROVE / MODIFY / REJECT

## Decision 4 — Separate GSU Representative Council

### Proposal

Create an elected GSU Representative Council that is **separate from the Local 6350 Executive Board**.

GSU officers and college/school representatives sit on the Council.

No GSU chapter office automatically carries a Local Executive Board seat.

### Recommendation

**Approve as proposed.**

### Why

Chelmsford separates its Representative Council from its Executive Board. This gives broad internal representation without unintentionally changing the balance of Local-wide governance.

**Leadership decision:** APPROVE / MODIFY / REJECT

## Decision 5 — College/school representation formula

### Proposal

Each college or school with at least five GSU members receives:

- one guaranteed Representative Council seat; plus
- one additional seat for every additional fifty GSU members.

The entire GSU membership also elects two at-large representatives.

For the first election, if reliable college/school membership counts are unavailable, use one representative per recognized college/school and begin proportional reapportionment the following election.

### Recommendation

**Approve the hybrid principle; adjust numerical thresholds if leadership prefers.**

### Why

It guarantees small academic units a voice while allowing larger colleges to receive additional representation.

## Decision 6 — RA/TA and MS/PhD interests handled through committees/caucuses

### Proposal

Do not create separate overlapping elected districts for:

- Master's vs. doctoral students;
- RAs vs. TAs;
- international graduate workers.

Instead, authorize GSU to establish standing committees/caucuses for these groups.

### Recommendation

**Approve as proposed.**

### Why

These identities overlap and can change between semesters. College/school constituencies provide a stable electoral base; committees provide formal representation for cross-cutting issues.

## Decision 7 — GSU controls its own bargaining team

### Proposal

The Local Constitution authorizes each bargaining unit to select its own bargaining committee.

GSU selects its bargaining team and develops its bargaining proposals through its own democratic process.

Only GSU members vote on ratification of a GSU contract, preserving current Article XV.

### Recommendation

**Approve as proposed.**

### Why

Both Chelsea and Chelmsford give bargaining-unit-specific officers defined roles in negotiations. Local 6350 already limits contract ratification to the affected bargaining unit.

## Decision 8 — Special elections for vacancies

### Proposal

### Recommendation

### Why

This minimizes unelected leadership while avoiding unnecessary elections immediately before a term ends.

## Decision 10 — Temporary summer / appointment-gap continuity

### Proposal

A temporary gap between covered Graduate Student Employees appointments — including a summer without a covered UMass Dartmouth appointment — does **not** by itself terminate GSU membership or eligibility to continue serving in elected GSU office when the member is expected to return to a covered Graduate Student Employees appointment in the immediately following academic term.

### Recommendation

**Approve as proposed.**

### Why

GSU terms run across the summer, but graduate funding and appointment patterns do not always do so. Without a continuity rule, an otherwise eligible elected officer could lose office solely because of an ordinary summer funding gap despite returning to the bargaining unit in the fall.

This rule does **not** cover graduation, permanent departure from UMass Dartmouth, or a member who is not expected to return to a covered appointment.

**Leadership decision:** APPROVE / MODIFY / REJECT

## Decision 11 — Contract-ratification administration

### Proposal

For a GSU collective bargaining agreement, GSU elected leadership participates in calling, administering, and communicating the ratification process **in coordination with Local 6350 officers**.

Only members of the Graduate Student Employees bargaining unit vote on the agreement, as the current Local Constitution already requires.

### Recommendation

**Approve as proposed.**

### Why

This gives GSU direct control and responsibility over communication with its own bargaining unit while preserving Local 6350's umbrella legal and administrative role.

**Leadership decision:** APPROVE / MODIFY / REJECT
