# Comparator Governance Analysis: Local 6350, Chelsea Local 1340, and Chelmsford Local 3569

Status: working research memo, not proposed constitutional language  
Prepared for: UMass Dartmouth Graduate Student Union / AFT Local 6350 amendment project  
Primary sources:
- `bylaws/source/Bylaw-Edits.md` — UMass Dartmouth Maintainers, Local 6350 Constitution (amended through April 2022)
- `bylaws/CTU Constitution, Amended 10-18-23.md` — Chelsea Teachers' Union, Local 1340 Constitution & By-Laws (amended October 18, 2023)
- `bylaws/CFT Constitution Bylaws 2.md` — Chelmsford Federation of Teachers, Local 3569 Constitution and Bylaws (document states amended July 21, 2010)

The comparator documents should be treated as structural examples, not as controlling law for Local 6350.

---

## Executive summary

The two comparator constitutions confirm that AFT locals can constitutionally combine:

1. Local-wide constitutional officers;
2. bargaining-unit-specific elected officers;
3. elected representative bodies organized by workplace/school;
4. unit-specific roles in bargaining and grievance administration; and
5. special-election procedures for vacancies.

The comparators point toward a strong working model for Local 6350:

- preserve a small Local-wide Executive Board;
- add an elected bargaining-unit officer for each major bargaining unit;
- establish GSU as a recognized bargaining-unit chapter;
- establish a separate elected GSU Representative Council organized primarily by academic college/school;
- keep GSU Representative Council seats distinct from Local-wide Executive Board seats;
- provide GSU substantial authority over bargaining, grievances, contract administration, and internal representation affecting its own unit;
- use special elections rather than routine appointment to fill GSU elective vacancies;
- do **not** copy the Chelsea three-year or Chelmsford one-year officer-eligibility rules for the newly organized graduate-worker unit without a transition exception.

This combines the strongest elements of Chelmsford's multi-unit structure with Chelsea's explicit bargaining-unit representation while avoiding structures poorly suited to graduate-worker turnover.

# 1. Local-wide officer structure

## Local 6350 now

Article IV currently establishes only:

- President
- Vice-President
- Treasurer
- Secretary

All are elected triennially by the Federation.

The Constitution does not define separate Local-wide and bargaining-unit offices.

## Chelsea Local 1340

Chelsea elects:

- President
- Vice-President
- three Paraprofessional Vice-Presidents
- Clerical Vice-President
- Treasurer
- Secretary

The bargaining-unit vice presidents are constitutional officers of the Local.

Their duties expressly include:

- communicating issues to the represented bargaining unit;
- maintaining communication with the Local President;
- representing their unit on the Executive Board;
- representing their unit in negotiations; and
- participating in grievance handling.

### Structural lesson

Chelsea demonstrates a simple model in which a Local retains ordinary Local-wide officers but adds elected officers dedicated to distinct bargaining units.

This is directly relevant to Local 6350 if the Local wishes to add a Graduate Student bargaining-unit officer without fully duplicating President/Vice-President/Treasurer/Secretary at the Local constitutional level.

## Chelmsford Local 3569

Chelmsford elects:

- President
- Executive Vice President
- one Bargaining Unit Vice President for each bargaining unit represented by the union
- Treasurer
- Secretary

Its Constitution specifically describes Bargaining Unit Vice Presidents as liaisons between their chapters and the union.

### Structural lesson

Chelmsford is the cleaner conceptual model for a multi-unit umbrella local because it distinguishes:

- an Executive Vice President serving the Local as a whole; and
- Bargaining Unit Vice Presidents serving each bargaining unit/chapter.

## Implication for Local 6350

The comparators support creating an elected GSU bargaining-unit officer at the Local constitutional level.

However, they do **not** by themselves answer whether GSU should internally elect a full four-person officer slate.

A workable two-layer model would be:

### Local constitutional layer
- Local President
- Local Vice President / Executive Vice President
- Local Treasurer
- Local Secretary
- Maintainers Bargaining Unit Vice President or Chair
- GSU Bargaining Unit Vice President or Chair

### GSU chapter layer
GSU could separately elect internal officers such as:
- GSU President/Chair
- GSU Vice President
- GSU Treasurer
- GSU Secretary

One of those GSU offices could simultaneously be designated by the Local Constitution as the GSU Bargaining Unit Vice President/Chair, preventing unnecessary duplication.

**Decision required:** choose the title and determine whether the Local-wide GSU seat is identical to the GSU President/Chair or a separate office.

# 2. Representative Council structure

## Chelsea model

Chelsea uses elected Building Representatives.

Each school receives one representative per 45 members.

The building representatives are part of the Executive Board itself.

### Strength

This gives workplace representatives direct governing power at the highest internal board level.

### Risk for Local 6350

If GSU creates many college/school representatives and every representative automatically becomes a Local 6350 Executive Board member, the size and voting balance of the Local board could change dramatically.

That could create structural tension between the Maintainers and GSU even if neither unit intends it.

## Chelmsford model

Chelmsford creates a **separate Representative Council**.

The Representative Council consists of:

- elected representatives from each building; and
- members of the Executive Board.

Each school receives one elected representative for each 30 members or fraction thereof.

Representatives:
- are elected biennially;
- serve as liaisons between members and the Executive Board;
- are first-line resources for contract questions, benefits, and grievances;
- support union elections and organizing/action work.

The Executive Board remains a separate body consisting of the elected constitutional officers.

### Structural advantage for Local 6350

This separation maps very well onto the GSU problem.

It permits broad democratic graduate-worker representation without automatically changing the balance of the Local-wide Executive Board.

## Working GSU adaptation

A GSU Representative Council could consist of:

- GSU officers;
- elected representatives from each academic college/school;
- any approved at-large representatives.

Possible primary constituencies:
- College of Engineering
- College of Arts & Sciences
- Charlton College of Business
- College of Nursing & Health Sciences
- College of Visual & Performing Arts
- School for Marine Science & Technology
- other graduate academic units with bargaining-unit members

The final list should be based on actual bargaining-unit membership and current University organization.

### Seat-allocation choices

Three plausible methods:

**A. One representative per college/school**
- easiest to administer;
- guarantees every academic unit a voice;
- can underrepresent very large colleges.

**B. Proportional representation**
- e.g. one representative per 25, 30, or 40 bargaining-unit members or fraction thereof;
- modeled directly on Chelmsford/Chelsea;
- requires reliable membership counts and periodic reapportionment.

**C. Hybrid**
- one guaranteed representative per college/school;
- additional seats once a college exceeds a specified membership threshold.

The hybrid model is likely worth serious consideration because it guarantees small colleges a voice while preventing large colleges from being severely underrepresented.

# 3. MS/PhD and RA/TA representation

Neither comparator organizes elected representation around overlapping employment/educational identities comparable to:

- Master's vs PhD;
- RA vs TA;
- research vs instructional work.

Their elected representative structures are instead based on stable workplace units: schools/buildings.

That reinforces the working Local 6350 concept:

- use academic college/school as the principal electoral constituency;
- use committees, caucuses, or designated at-large seats for cross-cutting identities and employment types.

Possible GSU standing committees/caucuses:
- Research Employees / RA Committee
- Teaching & Instructional Employees / TA Committee
- Master's Student Employees
- Doctoral Student Employees
- International Graduate Employees
- Organizing & Membership
- Contract Enforcement / Grievance
- Communications

This avoids giving the same individual multiple overlapping electoral districts while still creating formal routes for those interests to reach leadership.

# 4. Executive Board composition

## Local 6350 now

The Executive Board currently consists of:
- all elected officers;
- committee chairs; and
- chief delegate to the local labor council.

Because Local 6350 does not yet define GSU elected offices, "all elected officers" becomes ambiguous once GSU governance is added.

## Chelsea

Chelsea's Executive Board includes:
- all elected officers; and
- all elected Building Representatives.

This is the broad-board model.

## Chelmsford

Chelmsford's Executive Board consists of all elected constitutional officers.

Its Representative Council is a separate body.

This is the compact-board model.

## Working direction for Local 6350

The **Chelmsford separation is better suited to Local 6350** because there are two distinct bargaining units.

A compact Local-wide Executive Board could guarantee equal institutional access without making every GSU representative a Local executive officer.

The Local Constitution should enumerate Executive Board membership explicitly instead of relying solely on "all elected officers."

A possible structure for discussion:

- Local President
- Local Vice President / Executive Vice President
- Treasurer
- Secretary
- Maintainers Unit Vice President/Chair
- GSU Unit Vice President/Chair
- other Local-wide positions expressly designated by the Constitution

GSU Representative Council members would not automatically sit on the Local Executive Board unless specifically elected/appointed to one of those seats.

# 5. Bargaining authority

## Local 6350 now

Article V's Collective Bargaining Committee is designed entirely around the Maintainers:
- President
- Vice-President
- Secretary
- Treasurer
- Custodial representative
- Grounds representative
- Power Plant representative
- Trades representative

Article XV already protects an important multi-unit principle: only members of the bargaining unit covered by a proposed agreement vote on ratification.

## Chelsea

Chelsea's bargaining-unit vice presidents expressly represent their units at negotiations.

## Chelmsford

Chelmsford goes further.

Its Constitution provides that:
- the Teachers' Negotiations Committee has a specified representative composition; while
- Librarian and ESP Bargaining Unit Vice Presidents negotiate their own units' contracts with up to six additional team members.

### Structural lesson

A multi-unit AFT local can constitutionally place responsibility for a bargaining unit's negotiations in the hands of that unit's elected leadership/team rather than forcing every unit through one generic Local bargaining committee.

## Working Local 6350 direction

Article V should stop treating the existing Maintainers bargaining committee as the universal committee for every unit.

A cleaner model is:

- Maintainers bargaining committee: selected under provisions appropriate to the Maintainers unit;
- GSU bargaining committee: selected/elected under provisions appropriate to GSU;
- each committee prepares and negotiates proposals affecting its bargaining unit;
- Local-wide coordination occurs when an issue genuinely affects both units;
- contract ratification remains exclusively with the affected bargaining unit under Article XV.

---

# 6. Grievance administration

## Chelsea

Unit vice presidents participate in grievance handling.

## Chelmsford

Building representatives and bargaining-unit vice presidents work jointly with the Grievance Committee.

Representatives are specifically described as the first resource for workers who need:
- benefit help;
- contract clarification;
- grievance assistance.

## Working GSU direction

The GSU Representative Council could double as a steward/first-contact network.

College representatives should be trained to:
- identify potential contract issues;
- provide basic contract information;
- route grievances to trained GSU/Local grievance personnel;
- maintain communication between their constituency and GSU leadership.

The Constitution should avoid requiring every college representative to independently prosecute formal grievances unless training and authority are clearly established.

---

# 7. Vacancies and special elections

## Local 6350 now

Most elective vacancies are filled by Executive Board appointment, subject to later membership approval.

## Chelsea

All vacancies are filled by special election, except that the Vice President succeeds automatically to President.

## Chelmsford

Vacancies are normally filled by election.

Executive Board appointment is reserved for:
- July and August vacancies; or
- vacancies within 30 days of the end of the term.

Representative Council vacancies use the same general approach.

## Implication for GSU

Graduate-worker turnover makes vacancy procedures unusually important.

A routine appointment model could produce unelected GSU leadership for substantial portions of a short academic term.

A good working rule is:

- succession for President/Chair by the elected Vice President;
- special election for other GSU officer and Representative Council vacancies when meaningful time remains in the term;
- temporary appointment only when an election is impracticable because the term is nearly over or the University is outside its normal academic session.

The exact election notice period should be checked for federal labor-law/AFT requirements before final drafting.

---

# 8. Term length

## Local 6350
Three years.

## Chelsea
Two years.

## Chelmsford
Two years.

## GSU issue

Neither comparator has graduate-worker turnover comparable to UMass Dartmouth GSU.

A three-year GSU term is likely to create frequent vacancies because many graduate programs and assistantship appointments are shorter than that.

Possible approaches:
- one-year GSU terms;
- two-year GSU terms;
- two-year terms with staggered elections;
- a Local triennial cycle for Local-wide officers but a separate shorter GSU chapter cycle.

The Local Constitution need not force GSU internal offices onto the same term as Local-wide officers if chapter elections are separately defined.

---

# 9. Officer eligibility

## Local 6350
One year in good standing.

## Chelsea
Three years in good standing.

## Chelmsford
One year for elected office.

## Implication

The comparator language does **not** solve the GSU startup problem.

Copying either eligibility standard would prevent newly organized graduate workers from immediately staffing their own constitutional offices.

The amendment package therefore needs one of:

1. a permanent shorter GSU eligibility rule;
2. an initial transition exception;
3. authority for GSU chapter bylaws to set eligibility consistent with AFT requirements.

At minimum, an initial transition provision is necessary if GSU leadership is to be elected promptly after adoption.

# 10. Elections

## Chelsea
- secret ballot;
- special elections for vacancies;
- at least 15 calendar days' election notice;
- building representatives separately elected.

## Chelmsford
- Election Committee administers elections;
- at least 15 days' notice before nominations;
- nomination petitions;
- plurality election for most offices;
- majority/runoff rule for President;
- building representatives elected by their constituencies.

## Local 6350
- secret ballot;
- triennial July election;
- 15 days before nominations and 30 days before election;
- existing reference to chapter chairpersons elected only by members of their respective chapters.

## Working direction

Preserve Local 6350's strongest due-process protections while explicitly separating:
- Local-wide elections; and
- bargaining-unit/chapter elections.

Only GSU members should vote for GSU-specific officers and representatives.

The existing Article IX reference to chapter chairpersons is useful evidence that unit/chapter-specific voting already fits within the constitutional framework.

---

# 11. Committees versus elected representation

Both comparator constitutions distinguish elected representative roles from appointed/selected committees.

That matters for GSU.

The GSU Representative Council should **not** be created merely as another committee whose members can be appointed by the Local President.

If it is intended to democratically represent colleges/schools, its members should be elected by the people they represent.

Cross-cutting RA/TA, Master's/PhD, international-worker, communications, organizing, and similar bodies can be standing committees or caucuses.

---

# 12. Electronic meetings and modern administration

Chelsea's 2023 Constitution expressly authorizes regular, special, board, member, delegate, and committee meetings to be held electronically or in hybrid form under an electronic-meeting policy.

Local 6350 currently speaks primarily in terms of physical posting and meetings.

For a graduate-worker unit spread across colleges, labs, campuses, teaching assignments, and research schedules, electronic/hybrid meeting authority is worth considering.

This is a modernization issue rather than a prerequisite for recognizing GSU, so it should not delay the core October amendments.

---

# 13. Comparator provisions that should NOT be copied automatically

## Chelsea's three-year eligibility requirement
Poor fit for a newly organized graduate unit.

## Chelsea's broad Executive Board
Putting every GSU college representative on Local 6350's Executive Board could distort inter-unit balance.

## Chelmsford's one-year eligibility requirement
Still creates a startup problem for GSU.

## Chelmsford's exact one-representative-per-30 formula
Useful model, but the correct GSU ratio depends on actual membership distribution.

## School-district-specific provisions
Buildings, principals, school days, superintendent-level grievances, etc. require adaptation to a university bargaining unit.

## Any comparator dues formula
Local 6350 finance provisions must be reconciled with its own dues structure and AFT requirements.

---

# 14. Strongest provisions to adapt

## From Chelmsford
1. **Bargaining Unit Vice Presidents — one for each unit.**
2. **Separate Representative Council.**
3. Elected constituency representatives.
4. Special elections as the normal vacancy mechanism.
5. Bargaining-unit vice president as chapter-to-Local liaison.
6. Unit-specific negotiating teams.
7. Representatives as first-line contract/grievance resources.

## From Chelsea
1. Explicit constitutional bargaining-unit vice presidents.
2. Unit officers' guaranteed Executive Board rights.
3. Unit officers' express role in negotiations and grievances.
4. Special elections for vacancies.
5. Modern electronic/hybrid meeting authority.
6. A proportional elected-representative model as an alternative seat-allocation example.

# 15. Working governance architecture for discussion

This is **not final amendment language**. It is the structure best supported by the three constitutions reviewed so far.

## A. Local 6350

### Local-wide elected officers
- President
- Vice President / Executive Vice President
- Treasurer
- Secretary

### Bargaining-unit officers on Local Executive Board
- Maintainers Unit Vice President/Chair
- Graduate Student Employees Unit Vice President/Chair

### Local Executive Board
Small body consisting of specifically enumerated Local and bargaining-unit officers.

## B. Graduate Student Employees Unit / GSU

### GSU elected officers
Possible structure:
- President/Chair
- Vice President
- Treasurer
- Secretary

The GSU President/Chair could simultaneously serve as the constitutionally recognized Graduate Student Bargaining Unit Vice President/Chair on the Local Executive Board.

### GSU Representative Council
- elected college/school representatives;
- GSU officers;
- optional at-large representatives.

### Cross-cutting committees/caucuses
- Research Employees / RA
- Teaching Employees / TA
- Master's
- Doctoral
- International Graduate Employees
- Organizing & Membership
- Contract Enforcement / Grievance
- Communications

### GSU authority
Subject to the Local Constitution and AFT requirements, GSU should control or have primary authority over:
- selection of its bargaining committee;
- development of bargaining proposals;
- negotiation of its CBA;
- contract-ratification process for its bargaining unit;
- unit-level grievance/contract administration;
- internal GSU elections;
- GSU Representative Council operations;
- communications and organizing directed to GSU members.

# 16. Decisions that now need leadership input

The comparator research narrows the major choices to a manageable set:

1. **Title of the GSU Local-wide seat**
   - Graduate Student Vice President?
   - Bargaining Unit Vice President?
   - GSU Chair?
   - GSU President serving ex officio as Bargaining Unit Vice President?

2. **Does the Maintainers unit also receive a formally defined bargaining-unit VP/Chair?**
   A symmetrical two-unit constitution is cleaner than writing a one-off GSU exception.

3. **GSU officer terms**
   - one year or two years?

4. **Representative Council seat formula**
   - one per college;
   - proportional;
   - hybrid guaranteed + proportional seats?

5. **Which graduate academic units receive seats?**

6. **Which GSU offices sit on the Local Executive Board?**
   The compact-board model suggests one primary GSU seat, but leadership may prefer two.

7. **Initial transition election**
   How quickly after amendment adoption should the first GSU constitutional election occur?

8. **Eligibility**
   What minimum membership duration, if any, should apply after the initial transition?

9. **Finance**
   How are GSU funds, budgets, dues, reimbursements, and signing authority handled?

---

# 17. Drafting consequence

The next redline should not merely "add GSU" to a few nouns.

The strongest architecture is to amend Local 6350 so it explicitly recognizes bargaining-unit chapters and then:

- define Local-wide governance;
- define bargaining-unit representation on the Local Executive Board;
- protect unit-specific voting and bargaining authority;
- create authority for GSU's elected Representative Council and internal chapter officers;
- preserve Local-wide unity for common matters.

That gives GSU real democratic governance without making it a subordinate appointed committee and without turning Local 6350 into two unrelated organizations.
